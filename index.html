Professional Growth and Career Goals

These experiences have shaped my interests in software quality assurance, testing, troubleshooting, cybersecurity, and software engineering.

By reassessing projects from earlier coursework, recognizing their limitations, and implementing significant improvements, I enhanced my capacity to evaluate existing software and formulate practical solutions. This experience further strengthened my interest in identifying software defects, examining their root causes, and advancing software quality before issues adversely affect users. My professional principles include accountability, technical rigor, ethical judgment, lifelong learning, and responsible software development. After graduation, I plan to pursue a career in software quality assurance, software testing, or a related area of software engineering and cybersecurity, while continuing to develop my technical expertise.

Technical Knowledge and Problem-Solving Skills

My studies in data structures and algorithms improved my ability to assess computing challenges and choose solutions that meet performance, reliability, and functional needs. I learned about structures including vectors, hash tables, trees, and graphs, and how to assess the time and space complexity of algorithms. In CS 300, I examined vectors, hash tables, and binary search trees to understand how each affects data storage, lookup, and processing. This work showed me that choosing a suitable structure depends on factors such as memory use, computational cost, and the specific problem. It also emphasized how Big O notation helps measure algorithmic efficiency and describe expected performance.

My software engineering coursework taught me to take a structured approach to developing applications. I learned to interpret requirements, plan program features, spot possible defects, and assess software through testing and debugging. I came to understand that a program running successfully does not, on its own, prove that it is reliable; quality also depends on how it handles invalid inputs, unexpected situations, and failed operations. These experiences deepened my interest in quality assurance and troubleshooting, where I can investigate software behavior, trace issues to their causes, and strengthen system reliability. Through assignments and technical projects, I practiced reviewing source code, making improvements, and recording test outcomes. Together, these experiences reflect my growing ability to use software engineering principles to address practical problems.

My computer science studies broadened my understanding of database architecture and information management. I worked with Python and MongoDB, practiced writing database queries, and implemented create, read, update, and delete (CRUD) operations. These experiences showed me how databases underpin larger applications and how choices related to indexing, filtering, validation, and data retrieval influence system efficiency and dependability. I also learned that successful database design involves balancing performance, ease of maintenance, data integrity, and the needs of the application. Together, my coursework in algorithms, software engineering, and databases reflects my ability to develop and assess computing solutions while weighing technical trade-offs. This work aligns with Course Outcome 3, algorithmic problem-solving, and Course Outcome 4, using computing methods, tools, and practices to build effective software solutions.

Security Awareness and Quality Assurance

Cultivating a security-focused approach to software engineering has been a significant component of my academic development. My coursework demonstrated how software vulnerabilities may arise from inadequate input validation, insecure credential storage, weak authorization mechanisms, improper error handling, and flaws in application design. CS 410, Software Reverse Engineering, offered a practical setting in which to examine these risks. During a security assessment of an investment management application, I identified potential issues related to fixed-size character arrays, hardcoded passwords, incomplete authentication, unrestricted login attempts, inadequate input validation, missing authorization controls, and absent audit logging. Recording these findings and proposing remediation measures deepened my understanding of how security weaknesses can compromise confidentiality and integrity, as well as application reliability.

These experiences also underscored the close connection between cybersecurity, software testing, and quality assurance. Software may function correctly under ordinary conditions yet remain susceptible to unexpected inputs, unauthorized access, or failed operations. I therefore learned that thorough testing must evaluate both intended functionality and potential failure scenarios. This perspective has shaped my approach to troubleshooting and maintaining existing software. I now prioritize defensive programming, rigorous validation, meaningful test cases, and the early identification of weaknesses before they can adversely affect users. These practices support Course Outcome 5: cultivating a security mindset that anticipates vulnerabilities, addresses design flaws, and safeguards information and computing resources.

Capstone Accomplishments and ePortfolio Overview

My CS 499 ePortfolio comprises three enhanced artifacts that collectively document my development in software design and engineering, algorithms and data structures, and database systems. Each artifact was adapted from prior SNHU coursework and selected for its potential to support substantive technical refinement. Enhancing these projects involved examining existing functionality, identifying design constraints, implementing improvements, validating outcomes through testing, and documenting the work. Collectively, the artifacts demonstrate my ability to apply computer science concepts to practical challenges, along with professional communication, informed technical judgment, and a sustained commitment to software reliability.

The first artifact, the SNHU Investments Client Management Application, was developed originally for CS 410: Software Reverse Engineering and addresses the Software Design and Engineering category. I revised the C++ application by organizing client information into structured records, extending client-management capabilities, strengthening authentication and input validation, adding persistent data storage, and incorporating an audit history. These enhancements demonstrate my ability to improve an application's functionality, maintainability, reliability, and security while considering how design decisions affect system behavior and data integrity.

The second artifact, the Course Planner Application, originated in CS 300: Data Structures and Algorithms and corresponds to the Algorithms and Data Structures category. I extended the application by implementing a directed graph and depth-first search (DFS) to evaluate course prerequisites. The revised system identifies missing prerequisite references, detects circular dependencies, and generates prerequisite-first course sequences. I also assessed algorithmic complexity and documented nine test scenarios. This work demonstrates my ability to apply algorithmic concepts, assess data structure suitability, and build computational solutions to practical problems.

The third artifact, the Grazioso Salvare Animal Rescue Dashboard, originated in CS 340: Client/Server Development and represents the Databases category. I enhanced its Python and MongoDB capabilities by introducing database-side filtering by rescue category, strengthening CRUD validation, improving credential management, and assessing compound indexing. Tests using a synthetic dataset containing 10,000 records indicated reductions of approximately 79.39% to 97.08% in the number of documents examined by the evaluated indexed queries, relative to collection scans. Additionally, all eight automated Python unit tests passed. These findings demonstrate my ability to assess database operations through quantifiable evidence while acknowledging the constraints of performance measurements.

Taken together, the artifacts reflect complementary dimensions of my technical development. The investment application highlights software engineering, reliability, and security-oriented design; the Course Planner demonstrates algorithmic reasoning, graph traversal, and problem-solving; and the animal rescue dashboard showcases database development, query assessment, and application integration. In conjunction with my code-review presentation, supporting documentation, and professional narratives, these projects provide evidence of my progress toward the five CS 499 course outcomes. The ePortfolio further demonstrates my ability to organize and present technical work in an accessible, professional format for prospective employers and other audiences.
